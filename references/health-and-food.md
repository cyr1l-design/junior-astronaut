# Astronaut health and food

Sources: NASA **OSDR** (Open Science Data Repository) and the **Canadian Space
Agency** food toolkit, read 2026-11-13. Summaries below are in our own words.
Link the study ID (e.g. OSD-268) when the game cites one: `https://osdr.nasa.gov/bio/repo/data/studies/OSD-268`.

## Food grown in space (NASA OSDR)

- **OSD-268: Lettuce grown on the ISS (Veggie, VEG-01B).** Lettuce grown in the station's "Veggie" plant chamber was harvested and analysed for safety and nutrition. It's the real "space salad" study.
- **OSD-766: Tomatoes on the ISS (VEG-05)**, Dec 2022 → Mar 2023, **101 days**. Tomatoes grown under two light recipes: red-rich (90% red / 10% blue) and blue-rich (50/50). A matching crop on Earth copied the station's conditions with a 48-hour delay. Gameplay hook: **light colour/power spent on grow lights → food**.
- **OSD-581: Ryegrass grown in fake Mars soil** ("Martian regolith simulant"). Plants also make **oxygen**, which is why they matter for life support, not just meals.
- **OSD-412/413/414**: several leafy greens grown together in Veggie. **OSD-177**: a fungus infected zinnias grown on the ISS. Space gardens can get sick.

## Bodies in space (NASA OSDR)

- **OSD-195: 21 days of bed rest** changes human muscle. Bed rest is how scientists imitate low gravity on Earth. Hook: **skipping exercise costs muscle**.
- **OSD-918 → 926: mice flown on SpaceX-16.** Spaceflight caused body-wide changes similar to ageing; the study names muscle loss, bone loss, heart changes and immune problems as known effects of long missions.
- **OSD-317, OSD-337: space radiation** (the heavy particles from deep space) changes cells and tissues. Hook: **radiation adds up and has a real health cost**.

Most OSDR studies are on mice, plants or cells, not astronauts directly; say
"scientists found in mice/plants" when that's the case.

## Food facts (Canadian Space Agency)

- ISS crews get **fresh food deliveries every few months**. Further away (Moon, Mars) resupply gets rarer or impossible, so crews must **grow their own**.
- Space food must be **light, compact, nutritious and tasty**, packed to last the whole mission.
- Astronaut menus run **1,900–3,200 calories a day** depending on the person.
- CSA astronaut **David Saint-Jacques** tended the Veggie "Ponds" plant system in the ISS Columbus module.
