# Europa: what NASA knows

Gathered **2026-11-14** from NASA pages, NASA-mission papers and NASA's own
APIs. Every line names its source. **No one has proposed sending people to
Europa**, so any outpost or astronaut there is imagined and has to be labelled
that way in the game. The physics around them is real.

Status marks: ✅ checked against a NASA page or NASA data · 🔢 our own
arithmetic from NASA numbers · ⚠️ widely repeated, but not traced to a NASA
measurement (don't show kids as a precise fact).

**Signs the ocean reaches the surface** (each is a real, named discovery):

- **Carbon dioxide** in Tara Regio, a patch of chaos terrain, seen by the **James Webb Space Telescope** (Sept 2023). It likely came up from the ocean. ✅ [NASA, Sept 2023](https://www.nasa.gov/?p=519748)
- **Ammonia-bearing compounds** near cracks, found in re-read **Galileo** data from 1997 (NASA, 29 Jan 2026). Radiation destroys ammonia quickly, so it surfaced recently. ✅ [NASA science news](https://science.nasa.gov/blogs/science-news/2026/01/29/nasas-galileo-mission-points-to-ammonia-at-europa-recent-study-shows)
- **Water vapour plumes**: tentative. Hubble saw possible plumes (2013, 2016); Keck measured about 2,000 tonnes of vapour on one night out of 17 (2016, published 2019); Webb looked in 2023 and saw none. **Say "may", never "does".** ✅ NASA Europa facts ("possible", "not confirmed")

## 3. Radiation: Europa's real enemy

Europa sits inside **Jupiter's radiation belts**. Jupiter's magnetic field is
**20,000 times stronger than Earth's** and spins once every ~10 hours, sweeping
charged particles across Europa all the time. ✅ [JPL, Clipper radiation](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/how-nasa-is-protecting-europa-clipper-from-space-radiation)

| Fact | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Where it's worst | The equator, in two oval zones joined at their tips that cover more than half the moon. **Weaker toward the poles** | ✅ [JPL radiation maps, 2018](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/radiation-maps-of-jupiters-moon-europa-key-to-future-missions/) (Galileo and Voyager 1 data; Nordheim et al., Nature Astronomy) |
| How deep it damages | Near the equator it breaks up molecules down to **10–20 cm** of ice; near the poles, under **1 cm** | ✅ same |
| How a NASA craft survives | Europa Clipper keeps its electronics in a "vault" of aluminium, just under 1 cm thick | ✅ JPL, Clipper radiation |
| Clipper's dose | ~3 Mrad over the mission behind thin aluminium, cut to ~150 krad inside the vault | ✅ [NASA engineering slides, 2021](https://nepp.nasa.gov/docs/etw/2021/14-JUN-21_Mon/1130_McClure-Keynote-Europa-CL21-2225.pdf) |
| Why Clipper doesn't orbit Europa | A Europa orbiter "might survive only months"; looping around Jupiter and dipping in for flybys lasts years | ✅ Clipper FAQ |
| Dose on the surface | "**About 5.4 sieverts a day**", enough to make a person very ill within a day | ⚠️ widely quoted, but it traces to a 1999 textbook, not a NASA measurement, and other estimates differ a lot. Use only as "a deadly dose within about a day", with care |

**What this teaches, safely:**

- **Ice is a shield.** A few centimetres protects molecules; real shelter for a person would need far more. (How many metres of ice would make a person safe isn't sourced yet. Find a figure before the game shows one.)
- **Where you go matters.** Poles: less radiation. Equator: much more. That's real, mapped NASA data.
- **Machines struggle too.** NASA plans around radiation by shielding electronics and keeping close passes brief.

**Careful with solar storms (DONKI).** At Europa, the radiation comes from
**Jupiter's own belts**, not mainly from the Sun. Big solar eruptions do reach
Jupiter, squeezing its magnetic bubble and brightening its auroras (Juno saw
one in October 2024), but the game must not say "a solar storm made Europa's
radiation spike" unless we find a measurement showing it. DONKI fits honestly
for **the journey there** (deep space, where storms are the danger: see
[radiation.md](radiation.md)) and for **Earth's side of the radio link**.

From **NASA JPL Horizons** (keyless API), the real Earth–Europa distance for

| | One-way delay | Round trip (ask + answer) |
|---|---|---|
| **Today, 14 Nov 2026** | **44 minutes** (5.3 times the Earth–Sun distance away) | ~1 h 28 min |
| Closest in the data (1 Jan 2026) | 35 minutes | ~1 h 10 min |
| Farthest in the data (Aug 2027) | 53 minutes | ~1 h 47 min |

**Behind the Sun:** about once a year Jupiter passes behind the Sun as seen
from Earth, and radio contact becomes hard or impossible. In the data, within
10° of the Sun: **16 Jul → 11 Aug 2026** and late **Aug → 13 Sep 2027**. ✅
(Horizons sun-angle column; "contact is hard" is standard deep-space practice.
Cite a NASA page on solar conjunction before showing it.)

**A true constraint:** in an emergency, the answer from Earth comes back an
hour and a half later at best. The crew must decide alone.

## 8. Things that are false and must not appear

- Breathable air, liquid water on the surface, or rain. None.
- Confirmed plumes or confirmed life. Both unknown.
- Solar panels working "like on Earth": 25 times weaker.
- Fast chats with Earth: 35 to 53 minutes each way.
- Solar storms as the main radiation source at Europa: it's Jupiter.

## 9. Europa Clipper up close: what it carries, how it's going, what it can prove

Added 2026-11-14. Source unless noted: [JPL press kit, science](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/press-kits/europa-clipper/mission/science). ✅

**Its job, in NASA's words:** to find out whether Europa is *habitable*,
meaning whether it has what life needs. NASA says plainly that it "is not
designed to be a life-detection mission". **It cannot prove there is life.**

**What it carries** (nine instruments plus a radio experiment):

| Instrument | What it does, simply | What it can show |
|---|---|---|
| EIS (cameras) | Colour and 3D photos | How the cracks and ridges formed; whether the surface is changing |
| E-THEMIS (heat camera) | Finds warmer spots | Where water may be close to the surface or coming out |
| Europa-UVS | Reads ultraviolet light | What the thin air is made of; whether there are plumes |
| MISE | Reads infrared light | Which ices, salts and carbon-based chemicals are on the surface |
| ECM (magnetometer, on an 8.5 m pole) | Feels the magnetic field Jupiter stirs up in the ocean | How deep and salty the ocean is; how thick the ice is |
| PIMS | Measures charged particles around Jupiter | Cleans up the magnetometer's readings so the ocean answer is right |
| REASON (radar) | Sends radio waves through the ice | How thick the ice is; pockets of water inside it |
| MASPEX | "Sniffs" gas from the thin air and any plumes | What the ocean is made of, if it reaches the surface |
| SUDA | Catches dust grains and breaks them down | Whether the dust holds carbon-based chemicals or ocean salt |
| Gravity and radio science | Tiny changes in its radio signal as Europa's gravity tugs it | Confirming the ocean exists; what's inside |

**How it's going** (as of 14 Nov 2026):

- Launched 14 Oct 2024. ✅
- **Mars flyby, 1 Mar 2025**, 884 km up: it used Mars's gravity to change course, and the radar (REASON) passed its first full test. The radar is too big to test whole on Earth. ✅ [JPL](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/nasas-europa-clipper-radar-instrument-proves-itself-at-mars/)
- **Comet 3I/ATLAS, 6 Nov 2025**: the UV instrument watched this comet from interstellar space from a side Earth couldn't see, and saw oxygen, hydrogen and dust. ✅ [SwRI](https://www.swri.org/newsroom/press-releases/europa-clipper-instrument-uniquely-observed-interstellar-comet-3i-atlas)
- **Earth flyby, 3 Dec 2026**: its last gravity boost, then on to Jupiter. ✅ NASA mission page
- **Arrives April 2030**, then about four years of science and ~49 Europa flybys. ✅
- **Known worry:** before launch, NASA found some transistors were less radiation-hardy than expected; the team judged it safe to fly. ⚠️ secondary report ([Space.com](https://space.com/europe-clipper-transistors-vulnerable-radiation)); find NASA's own statement before citing.

**What it can and can't settle:**

| Can likely settle | Can't settle |
|---|---|
| How thick the ice really is, in many places (radar + magnetometer + gravity) | Whether anything lives there |
| How deep and salty the ocean is | What the seafloor looks like (radar can't see that far) |
| Whether plumes exist, and what's in them if it flies through one | Anything before ~2031: first Europa results come after April 2030 |
| Where the surface is warm, young, or has ocean chemicals on it | Anything about people living there (no crewed mission exists) |

**Today, nothing about Europa has been "proven" by Clipper.** Everything we
know comes from Galileo, Juno, Voyager and telescopes. Clipper is still on its
way. The game can say "Clipper will find out", never "Clipper found".

## 10. Extra facts the base design leans on (added 2026-11-14)

| Fact | Status |
|---|---|
| **The ocean is out of radiation's reach.** Radiation damages only the top 1–20 cm of ice; the ocean sits under ~15–29 km of it | 🔢 from sections 2 and 3 |
| **No sunlight reaches the ocean.** Anything powered down there needs power sent from the base or carried along | 🔢 follows from the ice thickness |
| **Ocean pressure is huge but lower than Earth's at the same depth**, because gravity is 13%. Just under the ice (~25 km down) it's very roughly 300 times surface air pressure; at the seafloor it could exceed the deepest point of Earth's oceans | 🔢 rough: density × gravity × depth. Recheck before showing a number |
| **Making oxygen from water costs electricity.** The ISS splits water into oxygen and hydrogen with electric power | ✅ standard NASA ISS life support; cite NASA's ISS life-support page before showing |
| **The Sun stays low near the poles.** Europa's axis is barely tilted, so near the poles the Sun skims the horizon, which is bad for flat solar panels | ⚠️ axis tilt not yet traced to a NASA source |
| **Tides follow the orbit.** Jupiter squeezes Europa once every 3.55 days, flexing the ice | ✅ NASA Europa facts (tides flex the surface). That plumes rise and fall with the tides is shown at Saturn's moon Enceladus, **not** at Europa: say "may" |

## 11. Heat, oxygen and exploring the ocean (added 2026-11-14)

**Heat**

| Fact | Status |
|---|---|
| In a vacuum there's no air to carry heat away; a base loses heat only by glowing it off into space and by conduction into the ice it touches | ✅ basic physics |
| **Every watt of electricity used inside a sealed base ends up as heat inside it.** Machines warm the room; power sent outside (a drill, a sub's battery, a radio beam) leaves with it | ✅ basic physics (energy conservation) |
| Deeper ice is warmer (about −160 °C at the top, near 0 °C at the ocean), but only by roughly 5–6 °C per kilometre. **Digging a few metres down gives no noticeable warmth**; it shields from radiation, not cold | 🔢 rough, from surface temperature and Juno's 29 km shell. Don't show the per-km number without rechecking |
| NASA spacecraft keep warm with insulation blankets, electric heaters and small plutonium "heater units" that give off about 1 W each | ⚠️ from memory, check a NASA page before using |

**Oxygen**

| Fact | Status |
|---|---|
| Water ice is everywhere, so oxygen on Europa is limited by **power** (to melt and split it), not by supply | 🔢 from sections 2 and 10 |
| Europa's surface makes ~12 kg of oxygen per second, ~1,000 tonnes a day, **but spread over the whole moon** that's about 30 g per square kilometre per day, and it drifts off as thin gas. A person breathes roughly 0.8 kg a day. It can't feed a base | 🔢 from Juno's 12 kg/s and Europa's area. The 0.8 kg/day breathing figure still needs a NASA source |

**Exploring the ocean: what NASA has actually studied**

| Fact | Status |
|---|---|
| Real plans use **robots, not people**. NASA JPL's **SWIM** concept: a lander on the surface, a "cryobot" that melts down through the ice while staying linked by a cable, then ~48 phone-sized swimming robots released into the ocean. A concept study, not a funded mission | ✅ [Phys.org on the NASA NIAC award](https://phys.org/news/2022-07-swarm-robots-life-ice-europa.amp); NASA NIAC page to cite |
| SWIM robots have so little power that **they may carry no cameras** at all, and they talk to the probe with **sound (ultrasound)**, since radio doesn't travel through water | ✅ same |
| On Earth, deep-sea **hydrothermal vents** support life with no sunlight at all: microbes feed on chemicals from the seafloor instead (chemosynthesis) | ✅ [NASA Astrobiology](https://astrobiology.nasa.gov/news/searching-for-life-where-the-sun-dont-shine-part-4/) |
| Europa's seafloor **may** have vents, heated by tides | ✅ NASA Europa facts ("could") |
| Another idea: life could gather on the **underside of the ice**, where chemicals from the seafloor meet oxidants coming down from the surface (radiation makes them up top) | ✅ [NASA Astrobiology](https://astrobiology.nasa.gov/news/could-shallow-biospheres-exist-beneath-the-icy-ceilings-of-ocean-moons/), a proposal, not a finding |
| **No life has been found on Europa.** Any discovery in the game is imagined and must say so | ✅ |

## 12. Day, night, Jupiter and digging (added 2026-11-14)

| Fact | Status |
|---|---|
| One Europa day = one orbit = 85 hours: about 42 hours of sunlight, then 42 of night | ✅ NSSDC orbit period |
| **On the Jupiter-facing side, Jupiter blocks the Sun once every Europa day, at local noon, for about 3 hours.** It happens every orbit, because Jupiter's shadow is wider than the tilt that could make Europa miss it | 🔢 from NSSDC distances: Europa moves 13.7 km/s, so crossing Jupiter's ~143,000 km shadow takes ~2.9 h. Find a NASA page on Europa eclipses before showing the number |
| On that side, Jupiter goes through **phases** like our Moon: "new" (dark) at noon, "full" at midnight, so nights there are lit by Jupiter | 🔢 geometry. Brightness not computed: don't put a number on it |
| From the far side, Jupiter is never seen | ✅ tidally locked |

| **There may be pockets of liquid water inside the ice**, shallower than the ocean, possibly under chaos terrain | ✅ NASA Europa facts ("possible lens-shaped lakes"). Depths not sourced yet |

## 13. What can actually happen on Europa's surface (added 2026-11-14)

Raw material for events. **Evidence level matters:** ✅ observed or NASA-stated ·

### The cryobot itself ([NASA, "Digging deeper"](https://science.nasa.gov/science-research/science-enabling-technology/digging-deeper-to-find-life-on-ocean-worlds/))

| Fact | Status |
|---|---|

**Watch out:** a sudden "radiation strike" like a solar storm on Mars is **not**
established at Europa. Europa's radiation is constant and enormous, with
regular ups and downs. A storm-style spike would teach something unproven.

### Choosing where to dig

- **No one knows yet where the ice is thinnest.** Juno measured one region (~29 km); Europa Clipper's radar will map more after 2030. A game map of thickness is imagined, but the **clues** scientists use are real: young chaos terrain, cracks with ocean chemicals, warm spots (what Clipper's heat camera hunts for), possible water pockets in the ice. ✅
- The best-known clue sites, **Tara Regio** (Webb's CO₂) and **Conamara Chaos**, lie at low latitudes, where radiation is strongest. ⚠️ latitudes from memory; check the USGS place-name list before showing.

## 14. Io, aurora and "purple storms" (added 2026-11-14)

| Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Io's volcanoes send material into space that ends up as radiation around Europa | ✅ Io's volcanic gas becomes a ring ("torus") of charged sulfur and oxygen that spreads out to Europa's orbit ([Hisaki/Tohoku summary](https://pparc.gp.tohoku.ac.jp/std2025_05ju02en/?lang=en)) |
| Io's particles visibly mark Europa | ✅ Jupiter's spinning magnetic field drives Io's sulfur into Europa's **trailing side** (the side facing backwards in its orbit), which is darker and stained with sulfur compounds; Galileo measured it ~30% darker ([study](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.11737); [Sack et al. 1992](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1992Icar..100..534S/abstract)) |
| Europa has an aurora | ✅ Hubble saw a faint **ultraviolet oxygen glow**, brightest **near the poles**, powered by Jupiter's magnetic field. It shifts as the field's angle at Europa changes, and sunlight barely affects it ([Roth et al. 2016](https://kups.ub.uni-koeln.de/28208); [Eos](https://eos.org/research-spotlights/hubble-gazes-at-europas-aurora)) |
| The aurora is purple | ⚠️ **No.** It's ultraviolet, invisible to human eyes, and faint. NASA's purple and blue aurora pictures are false colour. In the game, purple is an artistic choice and must be labelled as one |
| The aurora *is* the radiation | ⚠️ Not quite. An aurora is gas glowing where charged particles hit it: a **sign** of the particles, not the danger itself |
| An "aurora storm" from Io raises the radiation at Europa | ⚠️ Not established. Io has outbursts nearly every year, and Jupiter's own aurora flares when a solar storm hits (X-ray aurora up ~8×), but no one has measured a dose spike at Europa's surface. Say "may", or don't make it cause harm |

## 15. How real power sources behave (added 2026-11-14)

| Source | Behaves like | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Nuclear battery (RTG): plutonium heat turned into electricity, as on Curiosity and Voyager | A **steady flow** that fades very slowly (plutonium-238 halves every ~88 years). Can't be turned up for an emergency | ✅ standard NASA RPS facts; cite NASA's Radioisotope Power Systems page before showing |
| Small fission reactor (NASA's Kilopower tests, 1–10 kW) | A steady flow for years; its fuel is finite over a long mission | ⚠️ from memory, cite NASA's Kilopower/KRUSTY page |
| Solar panels | A flow that **stops at night and during Jupiter's eclipse**, and is 25 times weaker than at Earth | ✅ section 4 |
| Batteries | A **small tank**: they store power from the other sources for the dark hours | ✅ general engineering |
| Real cryobot designs | **Carry their own nuclear heat source inside the probe**; the cable carries data, not power. A base sending power down a 15–29 km cable is a game simplification | ✅ NASA cryobot page ("self-contained", nuclear); tether = fibre-optic data line |

**How real missions budget a steady flow** (added 2026-11-14):

| Fact | Status |
|---|---|
| **Curiosity on Mars runs on a nuclear battery, and its team still budgets power every day.** It charges batteries overnight, every device and **heater** counts, tasks get combined to save energy, and as the plutonium fades there's less energy for science each day | ✅ [NASA/JPL, Aug 2025](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/marking-13-years-on-mars-nasas-curiosity-picks-up-new-skills/) |
| **Moon bases are planned around reactors too.** NASA's July 2025 directive: a 100 kW fission reactor on the Moon by about 2030. A later (April 2026) White House directive reportedly asks for at least 20 kW | ✅ 2025 directive ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/nasa-boosts-plans-for-nuclear-reactor-on-the-moon/)); ⚠️ the 2026 figure is from secondary reports |
| **A red line is real.** Opportunity (a solar rover) lost power in the 2018 Mars dust storm when its batteries ran too low, and never woke up again | ✅ well-known NASA history; cite NASA's Opportunity page before showing |
| **What really hits the red line is the battery, not the reactor.** A nuclear source keeps giving its steady flow; if the base spends faster than that, the battery drains, and below a safe level the spacecraft shuts systems off to protect itself ("safe mode") | ✅ how NASA spacecraft fault protection works (Curiosity, Opportunity); cite a NASA page before showing |
| **Power and cold kill together.** Spirit (Mars, 2010) got stuck in sand, couldn't tilt its solar panels toward the winter Sun, had too little power for its heaters, and never woke up again | ✅ well-known NASA history; cite NASA's Spirit page before showing |

## 16. Science check of Cyril's event list (2026-11-14)

| Claim in the list | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Nature on Europa's surface gives heat or power (steam vents, warm plume edge, salts or minerals as fuel) | ❌ **False.** No warm vents are known; plumes are unconfirmed and would be cold vapour and ice grains in a vacuum; salts aren't fuel. On the surface, heat and power come only from the base |

## 18. How real missions end, per resource (added 2026-11-14)

| Resource | What really ends a mission | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Power** | Battery below the safe level → systems shut down to protect themselves; if it lasts too long, they may never wake (Opportunity, 2018) | ✅ section 15 |
| **Heat** | Too little power for heaters → equipment freezes and breaks (Spirit, 2010) | ✅ section 15 |
| **Radiation** | **NASA caps each astronaut's whole-career dose at 600 millisieverts.** Reach it and you don't fly again: a firm, non-violent rule | ✅ [NASA technical brief, Dec 2022](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/radiation-protection-technical-brief-ochmo.pdf) (NASA-STD-3001) |
| **Cryobot** | Locked in by refrozen ice, or the cable snaps when the ice shifts | ✅ section 13 |
| Abort | Real crews are brought home when a limit is crossed; missions are designed to abort safely rather than to lose people | ✅ general NASA practice |
